### Proposed English text for review

**Introduction, agricultural motivation and transition.**

> Grain-storage networks connect seasonal harvests with demand that is distributed across places and periods. Choosing where to open or expand warehouses affects how much grain can be held, how quickly it can be received and dispatched, and which markets the network can serve. The study relates the work required to construct and solve the resulting models to the quality and practical interpretation of the plans they return.

Use this as a compact alternative to the current overlapping opening and preliminary question. The separate R01 proposal can replace the middle transition if accepted. Preserve the central question. The optional grammatical correction for explicit approval is “How does computational scalability affect the optimal resolution of grain-storage network design and hub location models?”

**Methods, attribution and physical meaning.**

> The investment formulation follows Rosa (2026) in its treatment of facility opening, expansion and bulk-handling conversion. Opening a candidate or expanding an existing warehouse can add static capacity, measured in tonnes of grain that can be held at a given time. Bulk-handling conversion increases the rates at which an eligible warehouse can receive and dispatch grain. These rates are measured in tonnes per day and translated into period limits. A warehouse can therefore handle more arrivals and departures while retaining the same limit on grain held in storage.

**Methods, experimental sequence.**

> Each run starts from a frozen workbook and ordered warehouse population. The input stage derives the effective costs, scenario trajectories and product-specific admissible network. The common Pyomo builder then constructs the model for Gurobi or HiGHS under the declared resource and stopping settings. After solution, the workflow exports investment and operating decisions alongside phase times and memory observations. Independent checks reconstruct balances and costs in the original units. The accepted solution and its bound provide the basis for interpreting service, handling and storage.

**Discussion, contribution and interpretation.**

> Reis et al. quantify the computational effort of tactical purchasing and transport decisions under uncertainty. Pourmohammadi et al. examine location and discrete storage sizing with demand fulfillment constraints. The present study examines how strategic warehouse investments and scenario-dependent flows behave when evaluated together with computational effort and independent solution checks. The reported gap applies to the penalized objective. Its interpretation therefore combines the objective certificate with domestic service, excess stock and reception overflow.
